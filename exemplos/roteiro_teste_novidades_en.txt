These cars are everywhere now. You see them at traffic lights, in supermarket car parks and outside the school gates. So let us look at what is really underneath them.

In March twenty twenty-three, B Y D sold its first car in Britain. Three years later, it has gone from five showrooms to well over a hundred and forty.

The B Y D Seal is the saloon aimed squarely at the Tesla Model Three. And the smaller B Y D Dolphin is the hatchback you will see in town. Both are fully electric, and both are surprisingly cheap.

In twenty twenty-three, analysts at U B S, the Swiss investment bank, took apart a B Y D Seal to see how it was built.

They spent weeks on it, piece by piece, and they wrote down where every single part came from.

About seventy-five per cent of the Seal's parts were made by B Y D itself.

That is the secret behind the price. U B S worked out that the Seal cost about fifteen per cent less to build than a Model Three. And remember, B Y D started out making batteries, not cars. Batteries for phones and laptops.

It is a reminder that every brand has problems. The question is how often, and how much they cost you when they happen.

In a survey of owners, one in five reported a fault in the first year.

That sounds like a lot, but most of those faults were small, and most of them were fixed quickly. Fewer parts is genuinely good news in one way. There is simply less to go wrong.

The biggest complaint was the infotainment system, at twenty-four per cent.

Screens freezing, the radio switching itself off, the phone refusing to connect. Annoying, but rarely dangerous, and usually fixed with a software update at the dealer.

One owner told us the repair cost more than two thousand pounds at the dealer, after a minor knock damaged the battery bracket.

And it was not only the money. Some owners waited a long time for parts to arrive from abroad.

Eighty-five per cent of the faulty cars were off the road for more than a week in the workshop.

There is one more thing you should know about the newest models.

I have not been able to confirm which cars sold in Britain use the newest battery pack.

Battery packs change quickly, and manufacturers do not always say which version goes into which car. So ask, and check the paperwork when you buy.

Now for the warranty, which is one of the better ones in Britain. According to B Y D's own British terms, you get six years of general cover, up to ninety-three thousand, seven hundred and fifty miles. The battery is covered for eight years, up to one hundred and twenty-five thousand miles.
